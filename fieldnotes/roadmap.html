A path through a living world

First explain one change. Then follow what it makes possible.

This route is for an experienced programmer developing practical Rust and ECS fluency. We'll spend time on ownership, borrowing, data modelling, and scheduling, introducing familiar syntax as it becomes useful. A value copied for an inspector becomes a way to reason about a meal proposal; an ordering edge becomes the difference between a rescue and a death. Those returns give the language ideas more than one setting in which to make sense.

Choose your route. All 28 numbered lessons are available, with short isolated labs and complete answers. The cumulative guides connect those ideas in one saved course project. You can read ahead or return to an earlier question without unlocking anything. Live Moss work remains governed by NOW.md.

Practical Rust and ECS fluency

Begin with A tick is a promise, where an observable state change gives mutation a purpose. Who owns the next change? follows the difference between copying a value, borrowing access, and moving ownership. Ask the world a typed question carries those permissions into components, resources, and queries, so a system's requested access also explains which entities it can visit.

Once you can find and change a value, its representation matters. Give the number a unit makes arithmetic assumptions explicit; Missing is a different state handles absence; and A rejected request has a reason explains invalid input through Result. Modules, collections, and traits then help those choices work together through useful interfaces.

Return to ECS access with disjoint query borrows, then ask when systems run in explicit schedule order. Deferred commands make visibility a separate question from ordering, and discriminating integration checks ask you to write tests that distinguish plausible mistakes. The bridge to the canonical movement task connects this reasoning with your live assignment. Along the way, the Rust terrarium lets you inspect the installed maintenance schedule through an actual compiled browser host.

Ecosystem depth

A meal is a useful next step because it changes two owners at once. Finite meals accounts for that transfer and resolves competition; target choice asks whether an earlier observation still permits an action now. Authored populations and individual costs supplies controlled comparisons before reproduction starts changing who belongs to the population.

Growth and environmental input adds food to the ledger, while lifecycle and history connects terminal outcomes with bounded records. Predation, rest, and reproduction each isolate a different eligibility or timing problem. Inheritance preserves where a child's value came from, and repeated experiments asks what comparisons can justify. These short labs make individual rules inspectable before you combine their consequences.

Use the continuing meadow after the core, meal, and growth work to connect upkeep, food, competition, and history in one saved Cargo project. The population extension adds paid births, maturation, and inherited upkeep. Then local observations and paid travel make reaching food part of survival, committed rest interrupts those journeys, and competing hunters makes one capture change the opportunities left in the tick. Each guide asks for whole-system decisions, keeps your earlier tests, and provides a complete worked checkpoint beside your implementation.

The refuge capstone then asks you to take more responsibility for the design. Choose how to represent shelter, write the complete membership system, and connect it to observation and capture at the right moments. A grazer entering shelter after a hunter has seen it makes the timing problem concrete. Once your implementation handles that encounter, design a matched placement experiment and decide what its results support. Staged hints and complete module answers are available, but your hypothesis and investigation begin with your own choices.

This course meadow is an authored game model you can investigate, separate from the live Moss simulation. Its implemented interactions don't select biological policies for your live project or establish scientific calibration. The compiled browser views accompany the route through hunting; the refuge extension uses native tests, owned reports, and a command-line experiment. Each gives you evidence about the part of the world it actually runs.
