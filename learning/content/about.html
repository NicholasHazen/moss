Make the page fit the way you study

This guide helps you choose a reading rhythm and keep the evidence you collect along the way. We’ll look at listening and focus controls, then saved notes, before explaining the different kinds of world shown in the course. You should leave knowing how to return to a useful passage and how to tell whether a result came from a teaching model, a Rust example, or the live simulation.

Read and listen together

Open Reading options above the chapter title whenever you want to adjust the page or listen. The controls stay tucked away until you need them, leaving the lesson itself at the center of the page.

Where generated narration is available, use the audio player or Play audio control in Reading options to listen with the current passage highlighted. You can seek within the recording, or turn off Follow highlighted passage while you inspect something elsewhere on the page. The player identifies how its recording was generated. These audio files are made from the course prose and saved with the reading edition, so playback works offline. They read prose and tables, but skip code and answer disclosures, so pause to work through those when the lesson reaches them. The page supplies the same prose in text.

Listen offers another route through your browser's speech service, with passage highlighting, pause, resume, and stop. Open a worked answer before starting if you want its prose included. Available voices and pronunciation depend on your browser and operating system; some voices may use a network service, although the Fieldnotes reading page itself doesn't send text to a hosted narration service. You can also use an external immersive reader if it better suits how you listen, select, and annotate.

Use Focus to hide the chapter navigation while keeping the reading controls available, and adjust text size to suit the page. The size is saved with your reading record. Models advance only when you request it, giving you time to compare their state with the explanation before taking the next step.

Accessibility checks and their limits

The controls use ordinary buttons, links, fields, and disclosures. Development checks covered the skip link, keyboard use of hints, focus mode, generated narration, and narrow reading layouts. A full screen-reader and assistive-technology compatibility audit has not been completed.

Keep a trace of your own thinking

Open Reading notes beneath a lesson to write down a question or mark your progress. A useful field note can begin with a prediction and end with the observation that changed it. To keep a passage beside that note, select the text and choose Keep selected text. This saves a quotation without changing the original lesson. You can mark a lesson not started, reading, practicing, or reviewed as a reminder of where to return; opening an answer or trying a model never marks Rust tests as passed.

Notes, saved passages, and progress stay in this browser on the same site address. They do not sync between devices or between the published course and a local copy. Keep longer research notes in your usual notebook if you want to use them elsewhere, and copy any writing you want to retain before clearing browser data.

Know which world you're observing

The JavaScript teaching panels make one idea easy to try: a tick versus a camera change, where a write lands, or which entities match a query. Their labels explain the represented rule and its omissions. For compiler and schedule behavior, the lessons instead provide original Rust examples and exact local commands, so you can bring the question to the tool that can actually answer it.

The evidence lesson's Rust panel runs a precompiled version of its Bevy exercise. You can compile your own edited file after its required tests pass, and the build label distinguishes it from the printed reference. The Rust terrarium uses a different source: the actual moss-sim crate, running the installed schedule in an independent world whose controls request ticks and read components.

The continuing course project has its own compiled views. Beginning with fieldwork, you keep one saved implementation through descendants, paid travel, rest, and predation. Those views let you inspect the interacting course rules without installing them in live Moss. The curriculum page places this route alongside all 28 numbered lessons and the bridge to the existing movement guide.

The refuge capstone extends that saved project through your own data, system, and experiment design. Its native tests and reports show whether protection works and how different placements affect the meadow. The existing browser modes continue to show their earlier scenarios without refuge configuration; rebuilding them from your new source doesn't add a refuge display. Keeping that boundary visible helps you choose the right evidence for a claim about your extension.

Native tests, schedule integration, and browser observations answer related but distinct questions. The edition records them separately so a passing calculation isn't mistaken for proof of the displayed result, and an appealing animation isn't mistaken for evidence that the intended Rust ran.

Learn from good sources, keep one coherent lesson

Fieldnotes is the project’s main book: an introduction to Moss, a practical Rust and ECS course, and a guide to developing the ecosystem. Earlier editions remain in Git history. Official Rust and Bevy documentation supports language and API claims, with links beside the relevant explanations. The source archive includes a SOURCES.md registry with versions, retrieval dates, permitted use, and why each source belongs in the course.

External talks and articles are most useful when you have a question to carry into them. The reading and viewing shelf pairs optional publisher-hosted talks and ecology context with original text routes back to a Moss experiment. Publisher-hosted media can be embedded when available, while the needed explanation stays in the lesson. Full copyrighted chapters and transcripts aren't copied into the course.

Reading tools are useful when they help you follow an explanation and remember why a result mattered.
