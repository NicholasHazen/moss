Model a population without changing old callers

The trace suggests three different things an individual must remember: whether it is juvenile or adult, when it may first act, and how it acquired its inherited upkeep rate. Choose representations for those questions before looking at the answer. Keep life state distinct from developmental stage, because a juvenile can die and a living adult can still be ineligible to parent. Also write down the invalid configurations your constructor should reject; those decisions define what the later systems may safely assume.

The worked design uses Stage, tick deadlines, and an optional BirthOrigin. Founders enter as adults without a birth origin in this run. A child retains its parent IDs, donor, copied value, proposed mutation, applied mutation, and resulting trait. The bounded type UpkeepTrait::new accepts values one through three, and that value becomes the individual's upkeep rate. Its actual payment still depends on how much reserve is available, just as it did in your earlier system.

These additions needn't change the meaning of an existing scenario. Preserve the public shapes Scenario, GrazerSeed, Snapshot, GrazerReading, TickLedger, and EventKind, then add CourseWorld::new_population(scenario, config) and population_snapshot(). Construction returns a validation Result; observation returns None when population mode is disabled. That distinction lets a caller tell an unavailable capability from an enabled population containing no animals.

Modelling hint: put agreement checks at construction

PopulationConfig::new provides defaults, but the world constructor must validate the complete config against its scenario. Founder trait records must cover exactly the scenario's grazer IDs, once each, and trait-derived upkeep must match the existing seed rate. Reject a duplicate, missing or unexpected ID, or a mismatched rate rather than silently changing the caller's scenario.

Complete validation contract

Require positive maturation delay, cooldown, contribution, and living cap. The founders must fit under the cap, two contributions must fit in the child's capacity, and a parent's debit plus the one-unit reserve floor must be representable. Birth cost may be zero. Require a nonempty mutation cycle containing only minus one, zero, or plus one. The first child ID must be above every authored grazer and patch ID; reject a configuration with no initial identity space left. The complete configuration module supplies the branches and error variants.

Complete learner test: tests/population_investigation.rs

Add this file beside your earlier tests once the population entry points compile. It checks that rejection preserves both current reports and the valid policy used by the next ordinary reset. The population-stage command later in the guide discovers this saved test.

use moss_course_ecosystem::{
    CourseWorld, FounderTrait, PopulationConfig, PopulationError, Scenario,
    SimId, UpkeepTrait,
};

#[test]
fn rejected_mutation_cycle_preserves_world_and_future_reset() {
    let scenario = Scenario::generous_supply();
    let config = PopulationConfig::new(vec![
        FounderTrait { id: SimId(1), upkeep: UpkeepTrait::new(2).unwrap() },
        FounderTrait { id: SimId(2), upkeep: UpkeepTrait::new(1).unwrap() },
    ]);
    let mut world = CourseWorld::new_population(scenario.clone(), config.clone()).unwrap();
    world.step();
    let before = world.snapshot();
    let population_before = world.population_snapshot().unwrap();
    let mut invalid = config.clone();
    invalid.mutation_cycle = vec![2];
    assert_eq!(
        world.reset_with_population(scenario, invalid),
        Err(PopulationError::InvalidMutationCycle),
    );
    assert_eq!(world.snapshot(), before);
    assert_eq!(world.population_snapshot().unwrap(), population_before);
    world.reset();
    assert_eq!(world.snapshot().run, before.run + 1);
    assert_eq!(world.snapshot().tick, 0);
    assert_eq!(world.population_snapshot().unwrap().config, config);
}

Write the complete stage system

With those states represented, implement mature from its responsibility: find living juveniles whose deadline has arrived and turn them into adults. The system must read the clock, identity, and life state, mutate the stage, and write a population event. Choose the complete query signature and traversal yourself before consulting the answer. As with upkeep, stable ID order keeps reversed spawn order from rearranging the journal and changing which records it retains.

The component stores matures_at_tick: Option<u64>. A founder has no scheduled maturation, while a child has a deadline; only a living juvenile at or beyond that deadline transitions. Changing its stage to adult makes the transition happen once, even though the deadline remains in the past on later ticks. Maturation records an event but adds no reserve, so a newly adult grazer still depends on the same stores it had as a juvenile.

Place visibility, action, and maturity on the tick

We now have three boundaries to place: the entity becomes visible, the individual may act, and the juvenile becomes adult. Follow the diagram through one step. Make one birth a complete transaction

The new systems can describe a child's life, but we still need to decide when a pair may create one. Each parent must be alive, adult, past cooldown, and able to pay while retaining one reserve unit. Both must share a contact ID that resolves to an actual patch. An empty patch can establish that contact; a missing ID or another grazer cannot. The worked policy sorts sites, sorts eligible parents within each site, and forms disjoint adjacent pairs, making the matching reproducible without promising fair access to a partner.

Before writing the committing system, construct an owned ParentView and a proposed BirthPlan. Work through reserves five and four under the initial policy: two units from each parent enter the child, and one more from each is dissipated. Then change the second reserve to three. Decide everything the accepted plan must contain, and everything the rejected attempt must leave alone, before opening the implementation.

Transaction hint: test the floor before debiting either parent

A three-unit debit must leave at least one. Calculate both parent remainders and cooldowns, the child's state and origin, identity advancement, counters, and journal operation before installing any part of the outcome. Otherwise a failure discovered late could leave one parent charged for a birth that never happened.

Full payment reasoning and implementation map

Five/four becomes two/one, with child reserve four and dissipated cost two. Five/three rejects because the second parent would finish at zero. Rejection consumes no parent reserve, child ID, birth event, or accepted-birth ordinal. In the complete reproduction module, plan_birth returns the proposed outcome; resolve_births re-reads candidates, obtains short mutable borrows for distinct parents, commits the plan, reserves a slot, and queues the child.

A queued child makes capacity accounting another part of the same transaction. Write a test with two funded pairs but only one available population slot: exactly one pair may pay, even before a query can see the first pair's child. Then try the accepted pair again in reverse order; its updated cooldown must prevent a second debit. For a rejected proposal, flush commands before checking that no child appeared, so an accidentally queued entity cannot escape the test.

The accepted child inherits the shared feeding site and receives an ID above every authored grazer and patch; IDs aren't recycled within a run. Checked preflight also covers deadlines, ordinals, and accounting, because an overflow discovered after payment would leave the same kind of partial outcome as an unfunded parent. The atomic boundary is this birth: rejection leaves earlier upkeep and earlier accepted births in the tick intact.

Make the inherited value affect an executed rule

A child in this model needs an upkeep rate as well as reserve. The first accepted birth copies the lower-ID parent's trait, the next copies the higher-ID parent's, and subsequent births alternate. Apply the next delta from the configured repeating cycle and clamp the result to one through three. Because only accepted births advance that ordinal and the child allocator, rejected attempts don't change the trait or identity a later successful birth receives.

Wire the resulting value into the child's actual maintenance_units_per_tick, then test its consequence. If the parents have traits one and three and the first delta is plus one, the child's trait should be two. On its first eligible tick without food, four reserve units should become two. An origin record could display the correct trait while the old default still controls upkeep; this test distinguishes that attractive but ineffective label from inherited behavior.

Inheritance answer: preserve the difference between proposed and applied

At trait one, a proposed minus one applies zero after clamping. Retain both values, the donor, and the resulting trait in the origin. The selected algorithm is upkeep-copy-cycle-v1; its next ordinal affects future births, so equal current reserves don't make two runs equivalent when their accepted birth counts differ.

This is a deterministic authored policy, not a random generator or a genetic model. Lower upkeep has no compensating disadvantage here, so a changing trait distribution cannot by itself establish adaptation or inevitable improvement. To interpret that distribution, look at who was born, who died, and which deltas the authored cycle supplied. A useful tradeoff would need an additional benefit and cost that the simulation actually executes.

Count lives without retaining every corpse

Births make a world of permanently retained lifetime objects grow without bound. Population mode therefore records a terminal outcome, removes the dead entity, and updates the roster before observation. The earlier mode keeps its original dead rows. In the population report, current readings describe living individuals, while founders plus lifetime births minus lifetime starvations gives the same census even after the bodies have been removed.

The resource account needs one addition too. A child's starting reserve was transferred within the world, whereas birth cost was dissipated. Across a completed tick, ending stores plus actual upkeep plus dissipated birth cost must equal starting stores plus accepted growth. Keep this assertion alongside the individual timing and parentage checks; moving the right total to the wrong child could satisfy the equation while violating the birth rule.

The living cap limits how much population the implementation and game policy allow. It doesn't measure the meadow's carrying capacity. Count an otherwise eligible formed pair only when that cap specifically blocks its birth; several blocked pairs in one tick produce several pair counts but one blocked-tick count. A full population with no eligible pair produces neither. Those counters help distinguish a flat census caused by the cap from one caused by food or missing partners.

Birth and maturation also need their own bounded journal, preserving the original event enum for growth, maintenance, meals, and starvation. The two journals fill at different rates and report separate complete intervals. Suppose base coverage is after tick four through ten, while population coverage is after seven through nine. A claim requiring both kinds of event is supported only for ticks eight and nine, where those intervals overlap.

Coverage answer: an origin is not an entire biography

A living child's fixed-size origin can still name its parents after their removal or the eviction of its birth event. It cannot reconstruct their missing events. Lifetime counters preserve census, while an interval query outside complete coverage returns unknown. Bounded journals and fixed-size origins retain useful evidence without recursively copying whole ancestry trees.
